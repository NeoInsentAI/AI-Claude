### LinkedIn
The EU AI Act doesn't care about your job title. It cares about what you actually do.

There's a critical legal distinction that most companies miss — and getting it wrong means assuming the wrong liability.

**Provider**: You develop, place on the market, or put an AI system into service. Even if you only customize or fine-tune an existing model, you're a provider for that modification.

**Deployer**: You use an AI system developed by someone else in a professional context.

Why this matters:

**Provider obligations:**
→ Design the system for compliance from the start
→ Full technical documentation
→ Conformity assessment before market entry
→ CE marking and EU database registration
→ Post-market monitoring and incident reporting

**Deployer obligations:**
→ Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA) before deployment
→ Designate competent humans for oversight
→ Maintain automatic logs for 6+ months
→ Notify workers before deploying high-risk AI in the workplace
→ Ensure staff have sufficient AI literacy

The twist: you can be both at once. If you buy an LLM and fine-tune it for your product, you're a deployer of the base model AND a provider of your customized version.

Many companies discovered this the hard way in early 2026 after misclassifying their role entirely.

What role does your company actually play? Have you mapped it?
