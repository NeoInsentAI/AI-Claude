# EU AI Act — LinkedIn Draft

---

Most companies think they have until August 2026 to comply with the EU AI Act.

What they don't know: building a compliant AI system takes 6 to 12 months. The window is already closing.

Here's what you actually need to know — without the legal jargon:

**Already illegal since February 2025:**
→ Emotion recognition in workplaces
→ Bulk biometric data scraping (Clearview-style)
→ Certain biometric categorization systems

**What kicks in August 2, 2026:**
→ High-risk AI systems (hiring, credit, healthcare, education, law enforcement) must be fully compliant
→ CE marking and EU database registration become mandatory
→ Human oversight mechanisms required across the board

**The fines if you're not ready:**
→ Prohibited practices already active: up to €35M or 7% of global revenue
→ High-risk AI violations from Aug 2026: up to €15M or 3% of global revenue

This isn't theoretical. In January 2026, the EU issued a formal data preservation order against X over non-consensual AI-generated imagery from Grok. Meta refused to sign the voluntary AI Code of Practice — and is now under heightened scrutiny from the EU AI Office. 56 European AI companies, including Mistral and Aleph Alpha, formally asked for delays. The answer was no.

---

Here's the part most coverage misses — especially if you sell AI to other businesses.

The Act draws a sharp line between **providers** (companies that build and supply AI) and **deployers** (companies that use it). If you're offering AI via API, platform, or service — you're the provider, and you carry the bigger legal responsibility.

What that means for you:

→ You must give your clients full technical documentation — model capabilities, limitations, known risks
→ You must publish a summary of your training data and show EU copyright compliance
→ You must provide integration guidelines and usage safeguards to anyone using your AI
→ For high-impact models: adversarial testing before deployment, incident reporting to the Commission within 15 days (2 days for severe cases), and log retention for 10 years

Your clients are responsible for how they deploy the AI. But design-level liability? That stays with you.

---

The Digital Omnibus package could delay some deadlines to late 2027 or 2028 — but it's not confirmed. Building your compliance strategy around a hoped-for delay is a gamble I wouldn't recommend to any client.

If you build AI, sell AI, or use AI in a regulated domain — the clock is running.
