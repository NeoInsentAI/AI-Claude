# EU AI Act — Facebook Draft

---

If you use AI in your business — or sell AI to clients — here's something most people aren't paying close enough attention to yet.

The EU AI Act is already in effect. Not coming soon. Already here.

Since February 2025, certain uses of AI are flat-out banned across Europe: workplace emotion recognition software, bulk biometric data scraping, certain categorization systems. These aren't future rules — violations are enforceable today, with fines up to €35 million or 7% of global annual revenue.

The next major milestone is August 2, 2026. That's when high-risk AI systems — anything touching hiring, credit, healthcare, education, or law enforcement — must be fully compliant. We're talking CE marking, EU database registration, human oversight mechanisms, the works. Fines at this tier reach €15 million or 3% of global revenue.

Here's the catch: compliance takes 6 to 12 months to build properly. If you're not already working on it, you're behind.

And this is real — not just policy talk. The EU ordered X to preserve all data related to its Grok chatbot in January 2026 after it generated non-consensual AI imagery. Meta refused to sign the voluntary AI Code of Practice and is now under closer regulatory scrutiny. 56 European AI companies, including some of the biggest names in EU AI, formally asked for a delay. They didn't get one.

---

One piece that doesn't get enough attention: **if you provide AI as a service to other companies, your obligations are significant.**

Under the Act, there's a clear split between providers (those who build and supply AI) and deployers (those who use it). If you're offering AI via an API, a platform, or a managed service, you're a provider — and the responsibility for the model itself sits with you.

In practical terms, that means:
- Giving your clients clear documentation on what your AI can and can't do
- Publishing a transparent summary of your training data and demonstrating copyright compliance
- Providing your clients with integration guidelines and usage safeguards
- For high-impact models: adversarial testing, incident reporting to EU authorities within 15 days (2 days for severe cases), and keeping logs for 10 years

Your clients own how they deploy the tool. You own what the tool is.

---

There's a possible delay on the horizon — a package called the "Digital Omnibus" could push some 2026 deadlines back to 2027 or 2028. But it's not finalized, and I wouldn't advise any business to count on it.

This is one of those moments where getting ahead of it is far easier — and cheaper — than reacting to it.
